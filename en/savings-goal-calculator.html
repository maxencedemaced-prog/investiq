How is the monthly amount calculated?

Without any return, it’s a simple division: what you still need, divided by the number of months. For 50,000 in 15 years starting from zero, that’s about 278 a month.

If your savings earn a return, each contribution grows until the deadline and the interest earns interest too, so you need to put in less. With a hypothetical average return of 5% a year, the same 50,000 in 15 years takes about 187 a month — nearly 16,000 comes from compound growth.

Time is your best ally

To reach 50,000 at 5% a year you need about 122 a month over 20 years, 187 over 15 years, but 322 over 10 years. Waiting five or ten years can almost triple the monthly effort.

Setting a good goal

  • A specific amount and date: “a 20,000 down payment in 5 years” is far more motivating than “saving more”.
  • An emergency fund first: keeping a few months of expenses available avoids dipping into your project at the first surprise.
  • An investment that fits the timeline: for a near goal, people usually favor safety; for a distant goal, some stocks or index funds may aim for more return, with ups and downs along the way.
  • Automatic contributions: a transfer scheduled on payday means you don’t have to think about it.

Frequently asked questions

Are taxes, fees and inflation included?

No, the calculation uses a constant gross return. To stay cautious, lower the return a little or aim slightly higher to account for inflation. This is not tax advice.

What if the monthly amount is too high?

Three levers: extend the timeline, adjust the goal, or start with a larger amount. Try different combinations — the effect of time is often surprising.
