What is dollar-cost averaging?

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) means investing the same amount at regular intervals, whatever the markets do. You buy more units when prices are low and fewer when they’re high, which smooths your average purchase price and removes the temptation to time the market.

Three scenarios, not a forecast

The calculator shows three constant hypothetical returns — 2%, 5% and 7% a year — to visualize a possible range. Real markets move irregularly and can fall sharply; results are not guaranteed.

Frequently asked questions

Is DCA better than investing a lump sum?

Historically, investing a lump sum at once has often done better on average because markets tend to rise over long periods, but DCA reduces the risk of investing everything just before a drop and is easier for most budgets. It’s a personal choice.

Are fees and taxes included?

No. Results are gross and before taxes. Not investment or tax advice.
