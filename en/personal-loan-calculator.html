How is the cost of a loan calculated?

Each month, you repay part of the amount borrowed plus interest. The higher the APR and the longer the term, the more interest adds up. The calculator uses the standard formula for a loan with fixed monthly payments, converting the APR into an equivalent monthly rate.

payment = amount × r / (1 − (1 + r)−months) with r = (1 + APR)1/12 − 1

Lenders don’t all compute APR the same way: in the EU and the UK it is an effective annual rate (as above), while in the US the APR is usually divided by 12. The difference is small, but your lender’s own figures always prevail.

Example: 1,500 borrowed at 8% APR over 24 months means a monthly payment of about 68 and about 124 in interest. Putting 68 aside each month, you would have the same amount in 23 months, with no interest.

Loan or savings: how to choose?

  • The purchase can wait (new phone, TV, trip): saving first costs less, and gives you time to check you still want it.
  • The need is urgent (a car you need for work, a broken appliance): borrowing can make sense if the payment fits your budget and you compare the APR of several offers.
  • Avoid stacking loans and “buy now, pay later” plans: after a few of them, it’s hard to know how much you owe each month.

Frequently asked questions

What is APR?

The annual percentage rate combines the interest rate and the mandatory fees of a loan (for example an origination or arrangement fee). It’s the figure to compare between two offers. In the EU it is often called APRC; in the UK, ads show a “representative APR”.

Is “buy now, pay later” free?

Not always: some plans are interest-free, others add fees, interest or late-payment charges. Read the terms, and add up what you already owe each month before taking on another one.

I already have several loans. Which one first?

In general, paying off the loan with the highest rate first saves the most interest. The debt payoff calculator shows you the difference.
