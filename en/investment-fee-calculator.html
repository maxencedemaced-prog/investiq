Why a 1% difference matters so much

Annual fees are taken from your whole balance every year, even in down years. Because they compound, a gap that looks tiny — 0.3% versus 1.8% — can add up to a large share of your final amount after 20 or 30 years.

Where to find the fees

  • United States: the fund’s expense ratio, in its prospectus or fact sheet, plus any advisory or account fees.
  • European Union / UK: the ongoing charges figure in the fund’s Key Information Document (KID), plus platform fees.
  • Broad index ETFs often charge around 0.03–0.5% a year; actively managed funds often 1–2.5%.

Frequently asked questions

Does a higher fee mean a better fund?

Not necessarily. Many studies show that most actively managed funds underperform their index over long periods after fees. This calculator only shows the arithmetic effect of fees; it says nothing about future returns.

Are taxes included?

No. The calculation uses a constant gross return minus fees, before taxes. This is not tax advice.
