Why small expenses matter so much

A small amount repeated often becomes a big amount. The math is simple: price × times per week × 52 weeks. A 2.50 coffee bought 5 days a week is 650 a year. A 22 takeout order twice a week is 2,288 a year.

yearly cost = price × times per week × 52

What if that money were saved?

The calculator also shows what the same amount could become if it were set aside every month, with an example return. That return is not guaranteed: a savings account pays little but doesn’t go down, while stock-market investments may earn more over the long run but can also fall in value.

Example: 650 a year, about 54 a month, saved for 10 years at 3% a year comes to about 7,600, of which about 1,100 is interest.

How to cut back without depriving yourself

  • Sort them out: keep the small pleasures that really matter, and cut the ones you do out of habit.
  • Set a weekly budget for these expenses: when it’s used up, wait until next week.
  • Move the difference to savings right away with an automatic transfer, so the money you saved doesn’t vanish into other spending.
  • Check your statements once a month to spot the leaks.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a coffee a day cost over a year?

A 2.50 coffee 5 days a week costs 650 a year, about 54 a month. Every day of the week, it’s 910 a year.

Should I cut all my small expenses?

No. The goal is to choose consciously, not to give up everything. Something that truly makes you happy has its place in your budget.

What can I do with the money I save?

Many people first build an emergency fund in an easy-access savings account. Money you won’t need for several years can then be considered for the long term: the compound interest calculator shows how it could grow (illustrative, not guaranteed).
