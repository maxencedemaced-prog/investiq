What is the 50/30/20 rule?

It’s a simple way to split your take-home pay (after tax) each month, without a complicated spreadsheet:

  • 50% for needs: what you have to pay whatever happens (rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, transport, insurance, bills).
  • 30% for wants: what you enjoy but could live without (eating out, going out, travel, clothes, entertainment subscriptions).
  • 20% for savings: your emergency fund, your projects, and paying off expensive debt early.

Example: with a take-home pay of 2,000 a month, the rule gives 1,000 for needs, 600 for wants and 400 for savings.

What if it doesn’t fit my situation?

It’s a rule of thumb, not a law. With high rent, a modest income or children, needs often take more than 50%. In that case, a 60/25/15 or 70/20/10 split is more realistic. What matters is to keep a share for savings every month, even a small one, and to increase it when your situation improves, for example with each pay rise.

The trick: pay yourself first

Set up an automatic transfer to your savings on payday, instead of saving “whatever is left” at the end of the month. You can then spend the rest freely, without guilt.

Frequently asked questions

Where do subscriptions go?

It depends: a phone plan or insurance is a need, a streaming service is a want. The subscription cost calculator helps you add them up.

Is a loan repayment a need?

The required monthly payment, yes. Anything you pay on top, ahead of schedule, belongs in the 20% savings. To see which debt to tackle first, try the debt payoff calculator.

Where do people put the 20%?

Many start with an emergency fund of 3 to 6 months of expenses in an easy-access savings account. For longer-term projects, the savings goal calculator shows how much to set aside each month. This is general information, not personal advice.
